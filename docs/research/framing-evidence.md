## Plain answer first

1. **Strongest single story: A renter in Fullerton, CA (Aug 2026).** Real house, real professional video tour copied from an older listing, $2,575 sent ($2,500 by Zelle). The house was real; the "landlord" was not. VERIFIED.
2. **Strongest "photos from the wrong apartment" story: BBB case.** A young man moving to San Francisco for a tech job wired $2,500 first month's rent; the real owner said "the interior photos were of a different apartment she had rented out earlier." That is exactly what SNAPBACK catches and reverse image search does not. VERIFIED.
3. **Primary numbers:** FTC: nearly 65,000 rental-scam reports, about $65M lost since 2020, median loss $1,000, ages 18 to 29 "three times more likely" to lose money, half start on Facebook, 16% on Craigslist (VERIFIED). FBI IC3 real-estate fraud (rental is inside it): $173.6M in 2024, $275M+ and 12,368 victims in 2025 (REPORTED, IC3 PDF not opened).
4. **CoStar cares in its own words:** "Zillow is building its rental business on stolen photos" (CoStar GC Gene Boxer, CoStar press release Sep 30 2025, VERIFIED). CoStar calls its library "the world's largest library of real estate photographs" and now owns Matterport.
5. **Honest framing correction:** in most cases the photos are real and from the right building (San Francisco S3, Fullerton S1, Pasadena S4). SNAPBACK's twin match proves _where_ a photo was taken; the CoStar record proves _who really manages it_. The pitch must say both: "the photos are real; the person is not." Photos-alone framing undersells it and invites "that's reverse image search."

## 3. CoStar angle (why the sponsor cares)

- **Stolen photos are CoStar's own fight.** CoStar sued Zillow on Jul 30 2025 over nearly 47,000 photos; Sep 30 2025 update: Zillow "has displayed at least 4,618 additional CoStar Group-owned images" and still shows "almost 8,000" of the originals. Gene Boxer (GC): **"Zillow is building its rental business on stolen photos. Tens of thousands of them, many stamped with our watermark."** Andy Florance (CEO): "Zillow's free ride on the agents' listings and CoStar Group's proprietary content is over." Source: https://www.costargroup.com/press-room/2025/zillows-ongoing-mass-infringement-costar-groups-intellectual-property VERIFIED (via Bright Data; curl 403).
- **They own the originals and the twins.** Same release: "decades-long investment in hiring photographers to create the world's largest library of real estate photographs"; CoStar describes itself as a provider of "3D digital twin technology" and lists Matterport and Apartments.com as brands. VERIFIED.
- **Later:** case moved to Washington state Dec 2025; a Mar 27 2026 filing claims about 53,000 images (realestatenews.com). REPORTED.
- **Apartments.com anti-fraud stance:** "CoStar Verified" badge for listings from known managers; reviews and removes suspicious listings; "pursues legal action". https://www.apartments.com/grow/about/avoid-scams-and-fraud REPORTED (page timed out on Bright Data).
- **Apartments.com listings are a scam source:** The Pasadena (S4) fake listing ran on Apartments.com too (KNX, VERIFIED). So CoStar is both the owner of the originals and a platform the clones land on.
- No CoStar executive statement on renter fraud specifically: NOT FOUND (searched "Apartments.com fraud listing scam detection CoStar Andy Florance renters scams statement", WebSearch, Nov 13 2026).
- **Pitch line:** CoStar already argues in court that a photo has an owner and a place. SNAPBACK turns that into a renter-side check: the photo knows where it was taken, and the CoStar record knows who actually manages that unit.

## 6. Three 15-second opening hooks (all built on real, cited cases; no invented people)

**H1 (boldest, recommended): "The house was real."**
"A renter in Fullerton, California found a home on Zillow near her family. The video tour was real. The house was real. She sent two thousand five hundred dollars by Zelle. The landlord was not real. Sixty-five thousand renters have told the FTC this story. This is the text she should have sent first." (cut to forwarding the listing by iMessage)
Sources: S1 (VERIFIED), FTC (VERIFIED).

**H2 (sharpest fit to the photo-snap moment): "Different apartment."**
"A new hire moving to San Francisco signed a lease and wired his first month's rent. The real owner later said the photos were of a different apartment. Every photo is taken somewhere. We can find where." (cut to photo snapping into the twin, or failing to)
Sources: S2 (VERIFIED). Say "a new hire", not a name; BBB gives none.

**H3 (scale and stakes, LA after the fires): "356 messages in eight hours."**
"Two months after the Eaton Fire, a Pasadena house that was not for rent got 356 messages in eight hours. Families who had just lost their homes were the audience. Renters 18 to 29 are three times more likely to lose money to this. Forward the listing. Get the truth back before you pay."
Sources: S4 (VERIFIED), FTC (VERIFIED). Careful: no named fire victim paid in these sources; say "were the audience", not "were scammed".

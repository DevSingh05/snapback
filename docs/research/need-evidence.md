## Plain answer

1. **Yes. It happens every day, it is growing in cost, and it hits young renters hardest.** Not a made-up problem.
2. **About 32 Americans a day report a rental scam to the FTC** (65,000 reports from Jan 2020 to Jun 2025, about $65M lost, median $1,000; FTC says this is an undercount). VERIFIED source, ESTIMATE per day.
3. **FBI 2025: real estate fraud complaints hit a three-year high, about $275M lost by 12,368 victims** (up from about $173M in 2024). VERIFIED via LA Times and Florida Realtors; IC3 PDF itself could not be parsed (UNRESOLVED at primary).
4. **43.1% of US renters have seen a listing they thought was fake; 6.4% (about 5.2 million renters) have lost money; 9.1% of renters aged 18 to 29.** Apartment List national survey (VERIFIED, but from 2018: say "a national survey", not "this year").

## 2. How the scams work in 2025 and 2026

- **Hijacked real listing, contact swapped.** FTC: "copying legit listings, changing the contact information so you reach them instead of the real landlord" (VERIFIED, framing run). LA Times: "Often, they simply take existing listings and repost them" (VERIFIED). Apartment List calls it "Hijacked Ads: A fake landlord posts advertisements for a real property with altered contact information. Homes for sale are often re-listed as rentals" (VERIFIED).
- **Photos or tour copied from an older listing.** Yeager's Google Drive tour "turned out to be a copy of a previous real estate agent's tour that had been downloaded" (LA Times, VERIFIED).
- **AI-made content.** LA Times: scammers post fake listings "sometimes using artificial intelligence to make the content appear legitimate" (VERIFIED). IC3 2025 report cited "the rise of AI" (quoted by LA Times, VERIFIED secondary). liv.rent: "generative AI tools to produce fake listings that look strikingly real" (REPORTED, vendor).
- **Owner "can't show it yet" or self-tour with a door code.** Yeager: tenants "wouldn't be leaving for a few more weeks" (VERIFIED). Houston (Aug 2026): man "sent her a lease agreement and gave her a code to get inside the home. She paid the man $750" (FOX 26, VERIFIED). Conroe: "Bailey gave her the code to get inside the house" (ABC13, VERIFIED).
- **Scammer abroad or untraceable.** Placentia PD: perpetrators use "burner social media accounts" and "difficult-to-trace platforms. Some are even operating from outside the country" (LA Times, VERIFIED).
- **Payment rails:** Zelle (Yeager, VERIFIED), wire (Tran, framing run), PayPal and Cash App (KHOU, REPORTED), cash (Milwaukee, VERIFIED), bank transfer (Reddit, VERIFIED). BBB's Napoliello names "prepaid debit cards, Cash App, gift cards or cryptocurrency" (FOX 26, VERIFIED).
- **Platforms:** Facebook about 50% and Craigslist 16% of FTC rental-scam reports (VERIFIED). Also Zillow (Yeager, Milwaukee), Redfin via MLS syndication (Houston $750), Apartments.com (Huberman), TikTok and Facebook (Placentia PD). No source gives a full percentage split beyond Facebook and Craigslist: NOT FOUND (searched FTC spotlight, flagmylisting stats page which says "no source we trust reports one").

## 4. Industry side (why CoStar and property managers would pay)

- **Big owners are already paying to fight cloned listings.** Invitation Homes (the owner of the Houston $750 house): "works with a third party to identify and remove fraudulent listings involving its properties" (FOX 26, Aug 2026, VERIFIED). On Conroe: "We are always frustrated to discover that one of our homes has been used for these types of scams, which can have a devastating effect on the victims" (ABC13, Feb 2025, VERIFIED). A paid takedown market exists: ListingDefense sells removal of "fake rental listing scams from Zillow, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist" (https://www.listingdefense.com/, search snippet, REPORTED).
- **Platforms carry the blame and the cleanup.** Zillow to Moneywise: "Our teams use a number of different tools to proactively prevent inappropriate content from publishing, and if a listing is found to be fraudulent after it's posted, our team takes steps to remove it" (VERIFIED). Redfin to FOX 26: "we have built a multi-layer approach to protect renters from fraud", yet the fake came in through MLS syndication (VERIFIED). Zillow to the LA Times: it removes listings "that make it past the company's screening process" (VERIFIED).
- **CoStar's own stake** (from framing run, VERIFIED): CoStar is suing Zillow over tens of thousands of stolen photos ("Zillow is building its rental business on stolen photos"), owns Apartments.com and Matterport, and Apartments.com runs a "CoStar Verified" badge (REPORTED, page timed out again this run). The Huberman fake ran on Apartments.com too (VERIFIED).
- **Landlord-side fraud costs are large too (different fraud, same trust problem).** NMHC Jan 2024 survey of 75 large owners: 93.3% saw fraud in 12 months; average respondent wrote off "nearly $4.2 million in bad debt", about 24.5% of it tied to fraudulent applications (https://www.nmhc.org/news/press-release/2024/rampant-increasing-fraud-impacting-rental-housing-costs/, VERIFIED). This is applicant fraud, not fake listings: use only to say "the industry already spends on verification", not as a rental-scam number.
- **Just Landlords (UK):** rental fraud is "a dual-victim crime; it leaves innocent tenants financially impacted while dragging down the reputation of honest, hardworking housing providers" (VERIFIED).

## 5. Existing solutions and why they fail

1. **Platform "verified" badges check the person, not the property.** Zillow told Moneywise its third-party identity-verification tool "does not confirm that they own any given listing"; the Milwaukee scammer had an "identity verified" checkmark (VERIFIED). That is SNAPBACK's exact gap: we check the unit and its manager of record.
2. **Takedowns come after the money moves.** Yeager's Zillow listing "was only up for a day", and she was contacted by text after it was pulled (LA Times, VERIFIED). Huberman's fake drew 356 inquiries in 8 hours (framing run, VERIFIED). NYU: 40% of cloned ads stayed up 20 hours (2016, via BBB).
3. **Syndication launders fakes onto trusted sites.** Redfin: the fake "came from a third-party multiple listing service (MLS), which then syndicated it to Redfin.com and several other websites" (VERIFIED).
4. **Seeing it in person and door codes do not prove ownership.** Tran toured in person (framing run); Conroe and Houston victims were handed door codes (VERIFIED). The advice "always see it in person" (Redfin, BBB) does not stop self-tour scams.
5. **Reverse image search finds copies, not truth.** It catches reused photos but not real photos of the right house posted by the wrong person (Yeager, Conroe, Milwaukee) and not interiors from a different unit with no prior online copy. Consumer checkers exist (FlagMyListing "Scam Checker", liv.rent verification) but rely on text red flags or platform-side ID checks (VERIFIED that they exist; their accuracy NOT CHECKED).

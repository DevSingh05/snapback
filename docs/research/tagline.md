# SNAPBACK tagline

Master Writer, goal snapback-tagline, Fri Nov 13 2026, 21:13 EST. Sources: decisions.md (21:15 tagline line), need-evidence.md, framing-evidence.md, angles.md, costar-answers.md (plain answer), artifact/snapback-brief.html (hero copy).

## Recommendation (read this first)

**Tagline: "Check any rental before you pay."**
**Subline: "Rental scams lie about the place, the person or the price. SNAPBACK checks all three against the building itself."**

Why: it gives Vidyut the breadth he asked for (any rental, every kind of lie) without promising to stop every scam. The promise is a moment (before you pay), which is true for every check we run, and the subline names the three rows the result card really shows.

## 1. Strategy (4 lines)

1. **Breadth comes from the shape, not a promise.** A rental scam has to lie somewhere: the place, the person or the price. We check all three, so the line covers every kind of scam without claiming to catch every one.
2. **For renters:** one habit at one moment, "check before you pay", because that is the last point where a loss can still be prevented (median loss $1,000, FTC).
3. **For CoStar and platforms:** the listing should match the building. Their data (record, Matterport twin, Vexcel) is the referee, which is what CoStar's judges need to hear.
4. **For judges:** every word survives a poke. We say "checks", never "stops all" or "guarantees", and the result card never says SAFE or SCAM, so the tagline must not either.

## 2. Ten candidates

Max 8 words per tagline, max 20 per subline. Honesty check on each.

### Renter-facing

1. **Check any rental before you pay.**
   Subline: Rental scams lie about the place, the person or the price. SNAPBACK checks all three against the building itself.
   Honesty: true. "Any rental" = any listing can be forwarded; the building-backed checks run only where CoStar has the building (demo: Houston), while the photo store, red-flag rules and presence challenge run on every listing.
2. **Make the listing prove it.**
   Subline: Forward any rental before you pay. SNAPBACK checks the place, the person and the price against the building.
   Honesty: true. The listing is checked against the building; the poster must pass the live presence challenge.
3. **Place, person, price. Checked before you pay.**
   Subline: Forward any rental listing. Get three plain answers about what matches the building and what does not.
   Honesty: true. These are the three result-card rows. "Plain answers" includes "could not verify", which we do say.
4. **Forward the listing before you send the deposit.**
   Subline: SNAPBACK checks the photos, the manager and the rent against the building. Free for renters.
   Honesty: true if the probe confirms rent and leasing contact fields (costar-answers q4). If not, say "the building record" instead of "the manager and the rent".

### Platform and B2B-facing (CoStar, Apartments.com, Homes.com, brokerages, managers)

5. **Every listing should match its building.**
   Subline: SNAPBACK checks photos, manager and rent against the building record, the 3D scan and the aerial view.
   Honesty: a principle, not a claim. Same rent and manager caveat as 4.
6. **Your listings are the bait. Check them first.**
   Subline: Scammers copy real listings. SNAPBACK checks each new listing against the building before renters ever see it.
   Honesty: "Your listings are the bait" is the brief's own section line and true (the Pasadena fake ran on Apartments.com). Listing-time checks for platforms are **planned** (auditor fix, not in the MVP): say "can check", or keep this for the roadmap slide.
7. **Your 3D scans can stop fake rentals.**
   Subline: The Matterport twin already knows every room. SNAPBACK uses it to check where a listing's photos were taken.
   Honesty: "stop" is too strong on its own; the photo-to-twin match is real but only for buildings with a scan. Use "can help stop" if kept. Weakest for honesty.

### Pitch-opening

8. **Every rental scam lies somewhere. We check where.**
   Subline: The place, the person or the price. SNAPBACK checks all three before money moves.
   Honesty: "every scam lies" is true by definition. "We check where" means we check those three places, not that we always find the lie. A judge may push; answer: "and when we can't tell, the card says so."
9. **The house was real. The landlord wasn't.**
   Subline: A renter in Fullerton, CA sent $2,575. SNAPBACK checks the person too, not just the photos.
   Honesty: true story (Fullerton, Aug 2026, VERIFIED). This is the old line in story form; good opener, too narrow for the tagline (Vidyut's direction).
10. **No deposit until the listing checks out.**
    Subline: Forward any rental. SNAPBACK checks the place, the person and the price against the building.
    Honesty: "checks out" can read as "proven safe", which the card never says. Usable as a spoken rule, risky as the printed tagline.

## 3. Top 3, ranked

1. **"Check any rental before you pay."** (recommended) Plain, six words, a stranger gets it in one read. "Any rental" gives the breadth; "before you pay" is the honest form of "prevented". Works for renters on the card, for platforms on a slide, and as the last line of the pitch.
2. **"Make the listing prove it."** The boldest and most memorable. It flips the burden onto the poster, which is exactly what the presence challenge and the twin match do. Needs the plain subline right after it, so best as the stage hook or the hero headline above option 1.
3. **"Place, person, price. Checked before you pay."** Most explicit about breadth: it names every kind of lie in three words and matches the result card one to one. Less warm, best for CoStar and Devpost.

Pairing to try on the realtor page: headline "Make the listing prove it." then tagline "Check any rental before you pay." then the Place, Person, Price subline.

## 4. The old line now

"The photos are real. The person isn't." leaves the hero (artifact/snapback-brief.html line 171 still uses it; master-designer should swap it). It stays as a **story beat**, not a promise:

- In the pitch, it is the turn after the Fullerton story: "The house was real. The tour was real. The landlord wasn't." It explains why the Person row exists and why reverse image search is not enough.
- In the problem section of the page and README, under "Every check looks at the photos. The photos aren't the lie."
- Never as the tagline, the Devpost one-liner or the title of the result card, because it describes one scam and Vidyut wants all of them covered.

## 5. Elevator pitch and stage opener

**Elevator pitch (2 sentences):**
SNAPBACK lets a renter forward any rental listing and checks the place, the person and the price against the building itself, using CoStar's record, the building's Matterport scan and a live check that the poster is really there. More than thirty Americans a day report a rental scam to the FTC, and our promise to each of them is the same: check any rental before you pay.

**15-second stage opener (44 words, about 16 seconds at 160 wpm):**
"Every day, more than thirty Americans report a rental scam to the FTC. Some saw fake photos. Some saw real photos and a fake landlord. Some saw a rent too good to be true. SNAPBACK checks all three. Check any rental before you pay."

Number source: FTC Data Spotlight, Dec 22 2025: nearly 65,000 rental scam reports from Jan 2020 to Jun 2025 (VERIFIED in need-evidence.md). "More than thirty a day" is our arithmetic (about 32 a day). If a judge asks, add: "and the FTC says that's an undercount."
